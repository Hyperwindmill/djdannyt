Who

Open format, strong roots.

Daniele Traverso, Genova. B-boy first: dancer, teacher, then the DJ at the jams and the training sessions. That is where the name came from — the scene gave it, he kept it.

Twenty years of nights in Genova, from hip hop and dancehall rooms to commercial open-format sets that run six hours without repeating a track. On a scene night a breaking cypher is a certainty, and DJing a cypher — right BPMs, breaks extended by hand, reading the circle — is a craft of its own. He may be the only DJ in the room who has it.

Musician since childhood: classical guitar from age six, then electric, a short life in a metal band. That is why distortion, melody and groove all sound like home in his productions.

On AI, plainly: the first album, Uncertain Identity, was written and directed by him and generated with AI — and declared as such on every platform. Since September 2026 the default is the other way round: tracks are hand-made in Ableton, his own voice included, and AI is the exception. Either way, it says so on the label.

The sound

One rhythm, three speeds.

The catalogue runs on a single rhythmic DNA — the dembow — at three tempos. It is one authorial thread and one bridge for the dancefloor: a set can travel from a dancehall block into techno without changing its walk.

~98 BPM

Dancehall

The scene's own groove. Vocal hooks, chopped and stacked, built for the room.

~110 BPM

Moombahton

Dembow with a melodic top — the crossover lane, moombahcore when the drop asks for it.

~127 BPM

Techno

A sped-up dembow kick that reads as techno. Industrial, dry, hand-played.

140 half-time

Dubstep

Guitar-flavoured bass music: reese basses, screech leads, the nu-metal reflex.

Boom bap

Hip hop beats

Classic beats for the Genova rappers — the lane the scene asked for first.

Breaks

For the cypher

Breakbeat and BOTY energy, call-and-response baked in for the circle.

Live

Twenty years of Genova nights.

Hip hop and dancehall rooms, commercial open format, jams and training sessions for the breaking scene. He arrives with vinyl and a laptop, plugs into whatever is on the table and covers the whole night.

Latest: DJ Kamo's open-turntable night in the Genova vicoli, September 2026 — the listed DJs did not show, he took the console at six and held it for the evening. The scene broadcast his return itself.

Formats he is good for: scene nights (hip hop, dancehall, the cypher), warm-up to peak in open-format rooms, breaking jams and battles (right BPMs, breaks extended live), club techno / bass sets built around his own records.

Tech rider

Small rider, no drama.

He brings

  • Pioneer DDJ-SB controller — the whole rig when travelling light
  • Optionally a Numark V7 motorised deck on top
  • Laptop with VirtualDJ and Serato, full library offline
  • Own audio interface for DVS: plugs into any mixer, with vinyl
  • Headphones, cables, phone hotspot as backup network

Or he uses yours

  • Any mixer with two free channels (line or phono) for the DVS setup
  • CDJs and similar: fine — a rekordbox USB stick or the laptop hooked up, just say which in advance
  • Turntables welcome, not required

Always needed

  • A stable table and one power socket
  • A booth monitor
  • 15 minutes of soundcheck before doors
  • A microphone on the mixer for the cypher
